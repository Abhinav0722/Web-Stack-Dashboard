#Basic_response
As an analyst, querying this specific dataset gives you an incredibly powerful lens into the hospital's revenue cycle and audit performance. Because you have a flattened table that includes both clinical indicators (like mcc_drg and cc_drg), financial metrics (like TotalPaidAmount and true_find_savings), and routing markers (billtypecode and revenuecode), you can build queries that immediately flag operational bottlenecks, audit vulnerabilities, and vendor ROI.
Here are three creative SQL queries anchoring on the revenuecode column, complete with the analytical perspective behind them. *(Note: Citations for standard revenue and bill type codes are provided in the explanatory text, not inside the code blocks).*
### 1. High-Cost Carve-Out Leakage & Vendor ROI
**The Idea:** High-cost carve-outs like surgical implants (revenue code 0278) and specialty drugs (revenue code 0636) are massive revenue drivers but are highly prone to billing errors and audit scrutiny. We want to know which of our audit vendor partners are the most effective at finding "true savings" when auditing these specific, high-risk revenue codes. We will use the HitRate concept you mentioned, alongside a "True Savings Yield."
**The Query:**
```sql
SELECT 
    vendor,
    revenuecode,
    COUNT(ClaimNumber) AS total_audited_claims,
    SUM(CASE WHEN finding = 1 THEN 1 ELSE 0 END) * 1.0 / COUNT(ClaimNumber) AS hit_rate,
    SUM(true_find_savings) AS total_true_savings,
    (SUM(true_find_savings) / NULLIF(SUM(TotalPaidAmount), 0)) * 100 AS true_savings_yield_pct
FROM claim_table
WHERE revenuecode IN ('0278', '0636') 
  AND auditresult IS NOT NULL
GROUP BY 
    vendor, 
    revenuecode
ORDER BY 
    total_true_savings DESC;

```
**The Insight:** This tells you exactly which vendor to assign to your most expensive claims. If Vendor A has a high hit_rate but a low true_savings_yield_pct, they are finding lots of minor clerical errors. If Vendor B has a lower hit_rate but a massive total_true_savings on implants (0278), they possess specialized clinical knowledge that you should leverage for complex surgical audits.
### 2. ICU Clinical Vulnerability Index (CVI) Mismatch
**The Idea:** Intensive Care Unit (ICU) services are billed under revenue codes starting with 020 (e.g., 0200 for General ICU, 0202 for Medical ICU). By definition, patients requiring ICU care are highly acute. If a hospital is billing for ICU days but the claim shows zero Complications or Comorbidities (cc_drg = 0) and zero Major Complications or Comorbidities (mcc_drg = 0), this is a massive red flag for an auditor. It suggests the hospital either inappropriately placed a low-acuity patient in the ICU, or the coding team completely failed to document the severe conditions that justified the ICU stay.
**The Query:**
```sql
SELECT 
    providerorganizationname,
    COUNT(ClaimNumber) AS vulnerable_icu_claims,
    AVG(lengthofstay) AS avg_icu_los,
    SUM(TotalPaidAmount) AS total_dollars_at_risk,
    SUM(true_find_savings) AS dollars_already_recouped_by_audits
FROM claim_table
WHERE revenuecode LIKE '020%' 
  AND mcc_drg = 0 
  AND cc_drg = 0
  AND billtypecode LIKE '011%'
GROUP BY 
    providerorganizationname
HAVING 
    COUNT(ClaimNumber) > 5
ORDER BY 
    total_dollars_at_risk DESC;

```
**The Insight:** This query immediately generates a targeted hit-list for your Clinical Documentation Improvement (CDI) team. If a specific providerorganizationname repeatedly appears at the top of this list with high total_dollars_at_risk, they are a prime target for a Medicare Recovery Audit Contractor (RAC) takeback. You can intervene proactively before the claim is finalized.
### 3. Emergency Room Boarding Friction & Financial Drag
**The Idea:** When a hospital lacks available inpatient beds, patients are often "boarded" in the Emergency Room for days. We can identify these claims by looking for standard Inpatient claims (bill type codes starting with 011) that contain ER revenue codes (045X). By cross-referencing this with the lengthofstay and financial fields, we can calculate the financial drag of poor bed management by specific providers.
**The Query:**
```sql
SELECT 
    providerorganizationname,
    COUNT(ClaimNumber) AS er_boarded_inpatient_claims,
    AVG(lengthofstay) AS avg_total_los,
    AVG(TotalChargedAmount) AS avg_total_charges,
    SUM(TotalChargedAmount) / SUM(lengthofstay) AS overall_cost_per_day,
    SUM(drg_vendorsavings) AS potential_audit_clawbacks
FROM claim_table
WHERE billtypecode LIKE '011%'
  AND revenuecode LIKE '045%'
GROUP BY 
    providerorganizationname
HAVING 
    AVG(lengthofstay) > 7
ORDER BY 
    overall_cost_per_day DESC;

```
**The Insight:** This goes beyond simple billing and enters hospital operations. If you see hospitals with high er_boarded_inpatient_claims paired with massive overall_cost_per_day and high potential_audit_clawbacks, it indicates that ER boarding is driving up the gross charges unnaturally. Auditors (represented by your drg_vendorsavings column) often flag these claims because the exorbitant ER charges on an inpatient claim look statistically anomalous compared to standard inpatient DRG baselines.


#Advance_response
Transitioning from basic reporting to advanced Fraud, Waste, and Abuse (FWA) detection requires shifting your analytical mindset. In FWA analytics, you are no longer just summarizing data; you are looking for behavioral anomalies, peer-group deviations, and systemic coding manipulations. Modern FWA platforms use advanced techniques to uncover suspicious sequencing, overutilization, and impossible clinical combinations.
Using your specific dataset, here are three advanced, complex SQL queries anchored on revenuecode designed to flag high-probability FWA behavior.
### 1. The "DRG Creep" and Suspicious Sequencing Detector
**The FWA Concept:** To maximize reimbursement, some facilities manipulate the order of diagnoses, elevating a severe complication code to the principal diagnosis (or indiscriminately stacking secondary MCCs) to drive up the DRG weight. We can detect this by utilizing our custom Clinical Vulnerability Index (CVI). If a hospital's CVI is drastically higher than the regional network average for standard Room & Board (012X) or ICU (020X) revenue codes, but their average length of stay (LOS) is unusually short, they are highly likely to be artificially inflating case severity.
**The Query:**
```sql
WITH ProviderMetrics AS (
    SELECT 
        providerorganizationname,
        COUNT(ClaimNumber) AS total_claims,
        AVG(lengthofstay) AS avg_los,
        AVG((surgdrg_count + mcc_drg + cc_drg) * 1.0 / NULLIF(lengthofstay, 0)) AS avg_cvi,
        SUM(TotalPaidAmount) AS total_paid_amount
    FROM claim_table
    WHERE billtypecode LIKE '011%' -- Inpatient Admissions
      AND revenuecode IN ('0120', '0200', '0201', '0202', '0203') 
    GROUP BY 
        providerorganizationname
),
NetworkAverages AS (
    SELECT 
        AVG(avg_los) AS network_avg_los,
        AVG(avg_cvi) AS network_avg_cvi
    FROM ProviderMetrics
    WHERE total_claims > 50 -- Establish a statistically significant baseline
)
SELECT 
    p.providerorganizationname,
    p.total_claims,
    p.avg_los,
    n.network_avg_los,
    p.avg_cvi,
    n.network_avg_cvi,
    (p.avg_cvi / n.network_avg_cvi) AS cvi_variance_ratio
FROM ProviderMetrics p
CROSS JOIN NetworkAverages n
WHERE p.total_claims > 50
  AND p.avg_cvi > (n.network_avg_cvi * 1.5) -- CVI is 50% higher than network average
  AND p.avg_los < (n.network_avg_los * 0.80) -- But LOS is 20% shorter than network average
ORDER BY 
    cvi_variance_ratio DESC;

```
**The Insight:** Truly severe patients (high CVI) physiologically require longer hospitalizations. If this query returns a provider, it means they are billing for critically ill, highly complex patients who somehow recover and are discharged much faster than the regional average. This is a classic signature of "Upcoding" and is a prime target for a Special Investigations Unit (SIU).
### 2. Phantom High-Cost Carve-Outs (Unbundling/Fabrication)
**The FWA Concept:** Revenue codes 0278 (Other Implants) and 0636 (Drugs Requiring Detailed Coding) represent massive, separate carve-out payments. A common abuse pattern involves billing for high-cost surgical implants or biologic drugs on claims where no corresponding surgical procedure actually took place.
**The Query:**
```sql
SELECT 
    providerorganizationname,
    ProviderTaxID,
    revenuecode,
    COUNT(ClaimNumber) AS phantom_carveout_claims,
    SUM(TotalChargedAmount) AS fraudulent_charge_exposure,
    SUM(TotalPaidAmount) AS fraudulent_paid_exposure,
    SUM(true_find_savings) AS historical_audit_recovery
FROM claim_table
WHERE revenuecode IN ('0278', '0636') 
  AND surgdrg_count = 0 -- No surgical DRG triggered
  AND proc1 IS NULL -- No primary ICD-10-PCS procedure code documented
  AND lengthofstay <= 2 -- Short stay, highly unlikely to involve complex implants
GROUP BY 
    providerorganizationname,
    ProviderTaxID,
    revenuecode
HAVING 
    COUNT(ClaimNumber) > 5
ORDER BY 
    fraudulent_paid_exposure DESC;

```
**The Insight:** This query isolates claims containing expensive hardware or drug revenue codes but mathematically proves the absence of any surgical procedure (proc1 is null and surgdrg_count = 0) to justify the implant. Providers surfacing here are either experiencing catastrophic coding breakdowns (omitting the surgical procedure code) or intentionally fabricating high-dollar supply charges.
### 3. Peer Outlier Detection via Window Functions (The "Overbilling" Provider)
**The FWA Concept:** When analyzing professional or facility data, analysts can find strong signals of FWA when a provider consistently exhibits substantially higher paid-per-patient totals for specific revenue codes compared to their direct peers. This query uses SQL window functions to calculate the cost-per-day for Emergency Room (045X) revenue codes and flags providers whose billing averages are more than two standard deviations above the state mean.
**The Query:**
```sql
WITH ProviderERStats AS (
    SELECT 
        providerstate,
        providerorganizationname,
        COUNT(ClaimNumber) AS er_encounters,
        AVG(TotalChargedAmount / NULLIF(lengthofstay, 0)) AS avg_er_cost_per_day
    FROM claim_table
    WHERE revenuecode LIKE '045%' -- ER Revenue Codes
      AND lengthofstay > 0
    GROUP BY 
        providerstate,
        providerorganizationname
),
StateAverages AS (
    SELECT 
        providerstate,
        AVG(avg_er_cost_per_day) OVER(PARTITION BY providerstate) AS state_avg_cost,
        STDEV(avg_er_cost_per_day) OVER(PARTITION BY providerstate) AS state_stdev_cost
    FROM ProviderERStats
    WHERE er_encounters > 20
)
SELECT DISTINCT
    p.providerstate,
    p.providerorganizationname,
    p.er_encounters,
    p.avg_er_cost_per_day,
    s.state_avg_cost,
    ((p.avg_er_cost_per_day - s.state_avg_cost) / NULLIF(s.state_stdev_cost, 0)) AS z_score_deviation
FROM ProviderERStats p
JOIN StateAverages s 
  ON p.providerstate = s.providerstate
WHERE p.er_encounters > 20
  AND p.avg_er_cost_per_day > (s.state_avg_cost + (2 * s.state_stdev_cost)) -- 2 Standard Deviations above the mean
ORDER BY 
    z_score_deviation DESC;

```
**The Insight:** Using a Z-score calculation (standard deviations from the mean), this query mathematically identifies facilities that are price-gouging or over-utilizing the Emergency Department prior to an inpatient admission. If a hospital has a Z-score of 3.0 or higher, it means their ER billing practices are statistically highly anomalous compared to every other hospital in their state, giving your audit team a mathematically defensible reason to initiate a targeted record request.
